Main findings (detail in sections 4–6):

1. **NSW Caselaw DOCX exports are not originals.** High confidence. All 203 probed exports share the same docProps, the same 198 `w:rsid` entries and the same base template. Their ZIP timestamps equal the publication date (Word writes 1980-01-01). Every field has been flattened. Footnotes do survive as real Word footnotes, so the citation text is still good evidence.
2. **NSW drafters use Word paragraph cross-references.** Medium confidence. 45 of 203 exports contain flattened hyperlinks to `#_Ref…` bookmarks that no longer exist. Most show only a paragraph or note number. This is the signature Word leaves after *Insert › Cross-reference*. Three exports carry `#_Toc` links, which point to a TOC field in the source.
3. **ACT published DOCX are fresh Word saves.** High confidence. All 10 have `revision=1`, a created date within days of the decision, template `Normal`, no tracked changes and no comments. They come in two house-template families, one per pair of chambers (section 4.2).
4. **Third-party marker: Dragon dictation.** Medium confidence. 7 of 10 ACT files carry `dgnword-docGUID` / `dgnword-eventsink` document variables. `dgnword` is the name of the Word add-in DLL for Nuance Dragon. No citation-tool marker (field, namespace, docVar or custom XML) was found in any sample.
5. **Courts do not follow AGLC4 subsequent-reference or pinpoint rules.** High confidence for NSW and ACT judgments.
   - Courts give no `(n N)` cross-references (0 across 213 documents) and seldom use ibid. Only 2 judicial officers used it, in 4 of 203 NSW documents.
   - Pinpoints are introduced with `at` (`[2014] NSWCA 319 at [175]`). The comma form appears in 2 NSWSC documents only.
   - NSW gives parallel citations report-first (`(1998) 193 CLR 72; [1998] HCA 11`). ACT gives them MNC-first with no report year (`[2013] HCA 37; 249 CLR 571`).
   - Short titles appear without quotation marks (`(Newey)`) or with double quotes (`(“Ibbett”)`).
   - Footnotes mostly hold references to the evidence and the record (`T 12.3-.8`, `CB 41[33]`, `PS [8A]`, affidavits, exhibits), not authorities.
6. **Most state judgments cite in the body text.** Only 19 of 203 NSW exports have any footnotes, and none of the 10 ACT files do. Footnotes cluster in NSWSC first-instance judgments and the industrial cohort.

## 2. Method

- Retrieval used `curl` against the official sites. Files are held, uncommitted, under `…/scratchpad/court/corpus/state/{nsw,nsw_probe,act,qld_pdf}/`, with `SHA256SUMS.txt` (41 listed samples). Original bytes are kept unchanged.
- Inspection used `…/scratchpad/court/inspect_ooxml_state.py`. A shared `inspect_ooxml.py` did not exist when this track started.
  - It reads the package as a ZIP and never executes anything.
  - It extracts: docProps (Application, AppVersion, Template, Company only), attachedTemplate (reduced to the basename), content controls, simple and complex fields (instructions rebuilt across runs), customXml root namespaces, tracked revisions, comments, VBA presence, hyperlink domains, bookmarks, styles and footnote/endnote text.
  - It never emits `dc:creator` or `lastModifiedBy`.
  - It also has a simple RTF branch (`\footnote` groups, `\fldinst`). No RTF was obtained, so that branch has not been exercised.
- Citation conventions were measured with `…/scratchpad/court/conv_stats.py`, a regex counter applied separately to body text and footnote text.
  - Counts include the cover-page “Cases Cited” lists (an NSW content control, the ACT style `Coverpage Authorities`), so the body counts overstate in-text use slightly.
  - The regexes miss some forms, for example judge names with internal capitals such as `McWilliam J`. Read the counts as indicators, not exact measures.
- A manual review read about 70 footnotes drawn from 7 NSW documents.

Observed forms (manual review; examples paraphrased to the pattern):

- **Full citation repeated in a later footnote** rather than a cross-reference (NSW-19, NSW-18: the same `(2019) 265 CLR 164; [2019] HCA 25` in two notes).
- **Footnote holds only the citation; the case name is in the text** (`[2026] NSWLEC 118.`).
- **Short title defined after the citation, then used as `Name at [n]`**, in body and notes (`… [2014] NSWCA 319 (Newey) at [175] (per Gleeson JA)`).
- **`At [53].`** used as a bare internal pinpoint to the judgment's own paragraphs.
- **Record pinpoints:**
  - transcript page.line (`T 294.18-.33`, `T 2.3-.8; T 4.31-.42`)
  - court book (`CB 3/29[20]`, `See CB pp 81-82`)
  - submissions abbreviations with paragraphs (`PS [8A], [9]; PSS [4]`, `DWS at [30]`, `FRS [38]`)
  - affidavits (`Affidavit, [deponent], 21 August 2026 at [9]`)
  - tender bundles (`R1, 12`)
  - transcripts (`Tcpt, 4 September 2026, pp 31(19) - 32(11)`)
- **Discursive notes**: non-publication order notes and explanatory prose. NSW-21: 60 notes of prose only (≥12 words, no citation) and 34 notes mixing prose with a citation.
- **Secondary sources** in NSW-21/22 use mixed styles (`Title (Publisher, City, Year), 39`; web pages with `<url>`). This is not AGLC4-consistent.

Interpretation: NSW and ACT judgments follow a court/judicial house style that departs from AGLC4 in several ways:
- no `(n N)` (AGLC4 r 1.4.1)
- `at` before pinpoints (AGLC4 r 1.1.6 forbids it outside r 1.4.6)
- parallel citations for Australian cases (AGLC4 r 2.2.7 forbids them)
- short titles not in quotation marks, or in double quotation marks (AGLC4 r 1.4.4)

See `../aglc4-rule-reference.md (outside the repo)` (r 1.1.6 at line 530, r 1.4.1 at line 635, r 1.4.3 at line 676, r 1.4.4 at line 697, r 2.2.7 at line 1598).

Confidence: high for the pattern frequencies, medium for “house style”. These are judgments; submissions to the same courts may differ (out of scope, OBI-R02).

## 8. Candidate enhancements (story refinements, evidence-gated)

1. **OBI-103: court pinpoint connector.** Add a profile option `pinpointConnector: "comma" | "at"` that applies to first and subsequent references. Evidence: NSW and ACT judgments use `at` about 99% of the time. Profile it as *judgment house style*. Do not apply it to submissions until submissions are sampled.
2. **OBI-103: parallel-citation variants.** Add `mnc-first-no-year` (ACT judgments). Keep NSW report-first. Flag the ACTSC preset as unverified for judgments. Note that the corpus shows judgments, while the presets cite practice directions for practitioners.
3. **OBI-102: short-title style option.** Add `(‘Name’)` (AGLC4), `(“Name”)` and `(Name)`, plus the subsequent form `Name at [n]`. Keep AGLC4 as the default.
4. **New story (Epic B): record references.** Add first-class “record” citations: transcript `T page.line[-line]`, court book `CB vol/page[para]`, exhibits, affidavits (deponent, date, para), and party-submission abbreviations with a defined-terms table. NSW footnotes are dominated by these (e.g. 356 transcript refs in one cohort).
5. **OBI-101/102: preserve Word cross-references.** 22% of NSW judgments show REF-to-paragraph cross-references. Obiter's refresh must not destroy `_Ref` bookmarks or REF/NOTEREF fields in a court template. Add fixtures with REF and TOC fields.
6. **OBI-201/202: discursive-note safety.** Keep user prose and non-publication notes outside managed citation ranges. 60 prose-only notes in one judgment.
7. **OBI-R03 tooling:** use the dgnword docVar as a documented “dictation” marker class. Do not treat it as evidence of a citation tool.

## 9. Gaps and next steps

- **VIC, SA, TAS, NT:** no original-format samples. AustLII and the NT site sit behind Cloudflare challenges. Workarounds were deliberately not used.
  - Next step: the user downloads 2–3 RTF/DOC files per court from AustLII in a browser, if AustLII offers an original format for them (not verified in this run). The files then run through `inspect_ooxml_state.py`, whose RTF branch is untested.
- **WA:** needs the eCourts conditions of use accepted. The user must decide on that, or download manually.
- **QLD:** PDF only (TCPDF re-render). Queensland Judgments offers no original format. Ask the Supreme Court Library Queensland through authorised channels (OBI-R04).
- **NSW:** originals cannot be obtained publicly. Template and tooling questions need OBI-R04 interviews or sanitised fixtures. ACTCA, ACT Magistrates and NSW Children's Court were not sampled.
- **Recency and diversity:** NSW and ACT samples are from Jan–Oct 2026. ACT has only 3 judicial officers, because the site lists just the 10 most recent judgments.
- **Measurement:** regex counts are indicative. A reviewed gold set of about 200 footnotes would firm up the per-court rules before any profile ships.
