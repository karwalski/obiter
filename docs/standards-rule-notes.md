# Standards rule notes: OSCOLA 5 and NZLSG 3

Derived, paraphrased rule notes for the STD epic (Standards and Court Mode Review). These are the rule authority for the OSCOLA and NZLSG feature-matrix fixtures; nothing here is transcribed prose. Each bullet gives the section number, a paraphrase and, where useful, a worked example in the guide's own citation form. Points that could not be confirmed are collected under "Unresolved" at the end and become DECISION-040 items.

Conventions: section numbers are the guide's own. "OSCOLA 4" notes mark where the 4th edition differs; where no such note appears the two editions agree on the point. Example strings are given exactly as the guides format them, minus italics (case names, book titles and other italic elements are marked in the note text where the rule is about styling).

## OSCOLA 5

### Footnote mechanics

- **§1.1** — Footnote marker after punctuation (inside brackets when it belongs to parenthetical text). Footnotes end with a full stop. Several citations in one footnote are separated by semicolons. Numbering restarts per chapter only in long works. Example: `Grondona v Stoffel & Co [2020] UKSC 42, [2021] AC 540; Henderson v Dorset Healthcare University NHS Foundation Trust [2020] UKSC 43, [2021] AC 563.`
- **§1.1.1** — When the case name appears in the text, the footnote omits it and gives only the citation. Example: `[2018] UKSC 4, [2018] AC 736.`
- **§1.1.2** — No footnote needed when the text already gives Act and provision; in footnotes drop the definite article. Example: `Human Rights Act 1998, s 2.`
- **§1.1.4** — Several sources for one proposition go in chronological order, separated by semicolons and with no "and" before the last; legislation before cases; primary before secondary; "See also" introduces less relevant sources in a new sentence.
- **§1.1.5** — Indicators (`See also`, `cf`, `eg`, `ie`) are roman, lower case except "See also", and take no punctuation after them. Example: `cf Donal Nolan, ‘Deconstructing the Duty of Care’ (2013) 129 LQR 559.` OSCOLA 4 had no dedicated indicators rule (new in 5th edition).
- **§1.2.2** — Cross-references name the footnote or the text: `See n 109`, `See text to n 20`. Never a bare "See above".
- **§1.2.3** — Latin gadgets (`supra`, `infra`, `op cit`, `loc cit`, `id`, `contra`) are avoided; `ibid` is now in that list. OSCOLA 4 §1.2.3 permitted `ibid` for the immediately preceding footnote (`ibid 6.`).
- **§1.3.1** — Minimal punctuation: no full stops in initials or abbreviations (`JG Fleming`, `WLR`, `DPP`).
- **§1.3.2** — Page ranges use as few digits as possible but at least two in the closing number (`1782–83`, `201–203`, `11–17`); paragraph ranges are never truncated (`[120]–[123]`); year ranges `1925–27`, `1989–2001`.

### Cases

- **§2.1.1** — Case name italic; everything after it roman. The `v` is part of the italic name. Components: name | medium neutral citation (MNC) | comma | best report. Example: `Corr v IBC Vehicles Ltd [2008] UKHL 13, [2008] 1 AC 884.` OSCOLA 4 §2.1.1 says the same and calls the MNC a "neutral citation".
- **§2.1.1** — Without an MNC: name | [year] or (year) | volume | report | first page | (court). Year in square brackets when it identifies the volume (`[1996] AC 155 (HL)`, `[2001] 2 AC 550 (HL)`); round brackets when volumes are independently numbered (`(2000) 3 CCL Rep 132 (QBD)`).
- **§2.1.3** — MNC is mandatory whenever one exists (from 2001 for HL, PC, CA and Administrative Court; 2002 for the rest of the High Court). Unreported: MNC alone. Reported: MNC, comma, best report. High Court MNCs carry their division in brackets (`[2009] EWHC 3340 (Ch)`); no court identifier is added after an MNC. Retrospectively created MNCs (pre-2001) must not be used (also §1.1.1; new in 5th edition, listed in the key changes).
- **§2.1.3** — A report covering several judgments lists each MNC chronologically, comma separated: `Masterman-Lister v Brutton & Co (Nos 1 and 2) [2002] EWCA Civ 1889, [2003] EWCA Civ 70, [2003] 1 WLR 1511.`
- **§2.1.4** — Best report order: Law Reports (AC, QB/KB, Ch, Fam) > WLR or All ER > specialist series. Heavily edited reports (newspapers, NLJ) only if nothing fuller exists: `Powick v Malvern Wells Water Co The Times, 28 September 1993 (QBD).` Unreported without MNC (always pre-2001): court and date in brackets, no word "unreported": `Stubbs v Sayer (CA, 8 November 1990).`
- **§2.1.4** — Series using case numbers keep that form: `R v Kelly [2008] EWCA Crim 137, [2008] 2 Cr App R 11.`; `Re G [2022] EWFC 55, [2023] 4 WLR 27.`
- **§2.1.5** — Court identifiers required only when there is no MNC and the case is post-1865: `HL`, `PC`, `CA`, `KBD`, `QBD`, `Ch D`, `Fam`. They come at the end, before any pinpoint. OSCOLA 4 §2.1.5 used `(QB)`, `(Ch)`, `(F)` and `(Com Ct)`; the 5th edition changed these (key changes, ch 2).
- **§2.1.6** — Paragraph pinpoints are square-bracketed numbers at the very end of the citation, after any report, with no comma before them; several paragraphs comma separated; spans with an en dash. Examples: `Callery v Gray [2001] EWCA Civ 1117, [2001] 1 WLR 2112 [42], [45].`; `Bunt v Tilley [2006] EWHC 407 (QB), [2006] 3 All ER 336 [1]–[37].`
- **§2.1.6** — Page pinpoints follow a bracketed court identifier with no comma; repeat the first page if pinpointing to it; several pages comma separated: `Beattie v E & F Beattie Ltd [1938] Ch 708 (CA) 720, 723.`; `R v Leeds County Court, ex p Morris [1990] 1 QB 523 (QBD) 530–31.` Unreported judgment without paragraph numbers: page after the bracket: `Ryde Holdings Ltd v Rainbow Corp Ltd (PC, 15 November 1993) 3.` OSCOLA 4 §2.1.6 added that a page pinpoint after a citation with no closing bracket takes a comma; the 5th edition omits that sentence (see Unresolved 1).
- **§2.1.7** — Judge named in round brackets after the pinpoint: `Arscott v The Coal Authority [2004] EWCA Civ 892, [2005] Env LR 6 [27] (Laws LJ).` Abbreviations: `Smith J`, `Smith LJ`, `Lord/Lady Smith` (UKSC), `Lord Smith MR`, `HHJ Smith`, `Smith DJ`, `JJ`/`LJJ` for plurals; no forenames unless two judges share a surname; no territorial qualification; no "as he then was".
- **§2.1.2** — Parties: first claimant and first defendant only; no forenames or initials for individuals; abbreviate `BC`, `Co`, `DPP`; `Re` for "In re"/"In the matter of"; `ex p` (no full stop); drop "and another"/"and ors"; keep `Ltd`, `plc`, `LLC`; keep descriptions like `(a minor)` only where the party is an initial. Example: `Re A (Children) (Conjoined Twins: Surgical Separation) [2001] Fam 147 (CA).`
- **§2.1.2** — Short forms: first-named party after a full first citation (`Phelps (n 14).`); ship name for shipping cases (`The Aliakmon (n 25).`); `R v Evans` may be `Evans` in criminal-law works even on first citation; popular name given once in round brackets with single quotes then used alone: `Mirage Studios v Counter-feat Clothing Co Ltd [1991] FSR 145 (Ch D) (‘Ninja Turtles case’).` then `Ninja Turtles case (n 12).`
- **§2.1.2** — Judicial review: pre-2001 `R v Lord Chancellor, ex p Witham [1998] QB 575 (QBD).` then `Witham (n 1) 576.`; from 2001 `R (Roberts) v Parole Board [2004] EWCA Civ 1031, [2005] QB 410.` then `Roberts (n 1) 410.`
- **§2.1.2** — Attorney General's references: `Attorney General’s Reference (No 3 of 2004) (R v H) [2005] EWCA Crim 1882.`; `A-G` acceptable in later references. Different report names joined with `sub nom`.
- **§2.1.8** — Subsequent history with `affd` / `revd`: `Roberts v Gable [2006] EWHC 1025 (QB), [2006] EMLR 23, affd [2007] EWCA Civ 721, [2008] QB 502.`
- **§2.1.9** — Pre-1865 cases: nominate report then English Reports, comma separated (semicolon when each has a pinpoint): `Boulton v Jones (1857) 2 H&N 564, 157 ER 232.`; `Henly v Mayor of Lyme (1828) 5 Bing 91, 107; 130 ER 995, 1001.`
- **§2.2.1 (Scotland)** — MNCs from 2005 on the E&W model (`CSIH`, `CSOH`, `HCJAC`, `HCJT`, `SAC Civ`, `SAC Crim`; appendix §5.1.3). Best report Session Cases (`SC`, `JC`, `SC (HL)`, `SC (UKSC)`), then `SLT`, then `SCLR`/`SCCR`. Year has no brackets when it locates the case (`2003 SC 171`), round brackets when volumes are numbered (`(1842) 4 D 1168`). Add `(IH)` or `(OH)` for the Court of Session. Pinpoint as for E&W. Examples: `Adams v Advocate General 2003 SC 171 (OH) 175.`; `Davidson v Scottish Ministers [2005] UKHL 74, 2006 SC (HL) 41 [10].`; `Smart v HM Advocate [2006] HCJAC 12, 2006 JC 119 [23]–[24].`; `Ponticelli Ltd v Gallagher [2023] CSIH 32, 2023 SLT 823.`
- **§2.3.1 (Northern Ireland)** — MNCs on the E&W model (`NICA`, `NIKB`, `NIQB`, `NIFam`, `NICh`, `NICC`, `NIMaster`; appendix §5.1.4); court identifier when there is no MNC; NI reports from 1925, Irish Reports before. Examples: `Hylands v McClintock [1999] NI 28 (Ch D).`; `Wilson v Commissioner of Valuation [2009] NICA 30, [2010] NI 48.`
- **§2.6.1** — Other jurisdictions: cite as at home but with no full stops; add the court in brackets if the series does not show it. Examples: `Harriton v Stephens [2006] HCA 15, (2006) 226 CLR 52.`; `Brown v New Zealand Basing Ltd [2018] 1 NZLR 245 (SC).`; `Kerr v Baranow 2011 SCC 10, [2011] 1 SCR 269.`

### Legislation

- **§2.4.1** — Act cited by short title and year, roman (never italic), no comma before the year, no "the" in footnotes; jurisdiction in brackets only where several jurisdictions are discussed: `Water Resources Act 1991 (UK).`; `Civil Liability Act 2002 (NSW).`
- **§2.4.2** — Labels: `pt/pts`, `s/ss`, `sub-s/sub-ss`, `para/paras`, `subpara/subparas`, `sch/schs`. In footnotes a comma after the year and a space after the label: `Criminal Attempts Act 1981, ss 1(1) and 4(3).`; `Sexual Offences Act 2003, s 1(1)(c).` In text: `Section 5(1)(a) of the Race Relations Act 1976`, `s 11(1A) of the Limitation Act 1980`, `sub-s (3)`.
- **§2.4.3** — Older statutes may add regnal year and chapter: `Crown Debts Act 1801 (41 Geo 3 c 90).`
- **§2.4.4** — Explanatory notes: `Explanatory Notes to the Charities Act 2006, para 15.`
- **§2.4.5** — Bills: title | `HC Bill`/`HL Bill` | (session) | number (square brackets for Commons, none for Lords); clauses `cl`/`cls`: `Consolidated Fund HC Bill (2008–2009) [5].`; `Academies HL Bill (2010–11) 1, cl 8(2).`
- **§2.4.6–2.4.8** — Wales: `(asc 2)` post-2020 Senedd Acts, `(anaw 3)` Assembly Acts, `(nawm 2)` pre-2011 Measures; Scotland `(asp 7)`; NI `Act (NI) 1968` for the old Parliament, `Act (Northern Ireland) 2009` for the Assembly. Welsh and NI bills carry the stage in square brackets: `Budget Bill (Northern Ireland) [as introduced] 2020.` (Welsh legislation §2.4.6 and NI bills §2.4.8 are new in the 5th edition.)
- **§2.4.9** — Assimilated EU law (new): prefix `Assimilated`, replace the OJ reference with the amending SI: `Assimilated Regulation (EC) No 593/2008 … on the law applicable to contractual obligations, as amended by SI 2019/834, reg 10 (‘Rome I Regulation (Assimilated)’).`
- **§2.5.1** — Statutory instruments: name, year, comma, `SI year/number`: `Russia (Sanctions) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019, SI 2019/855.` Older: `SR & O 1921/2032`.
- **§2.5.2** — SI parts add `art/arts`, `reg/regs`, `r/rr`: `Eggs and Chicks (England) Regulations 2009, SI 2009/2163, reg 7(2).`
- **§2.5.3** — Rules of court without SI number or year: `CPR 5.2(1)(b).`; `RSC Ord 24 r 14A.`; `CPR PD 52C.`
- **§2.5.4–2.5.6** — Welsh SIs add `(W 245)`; Scottish `SSI 2003/387`; NI statutory rules `SR 2022/50`.
- **§2.6.2** — Foreign legislation as at home without full stops, jurisdiction in brackets if needed: `Accident Compensation Act 1972 (NZ).`; `Climate Change Act 2022 (Cth).`

### Subsequent references

- **§1.2.1** — Subsequent citation = brief identifier + `(n X)` cross-citation to the footnote with the full citation; then the pinpoint with no comma. `ibid` is not used (removed; key changes ch 1). Case: `Austin (n 1) [34], [39], [43]–[47].` Book: `Stevens (n 1) 110.` OSCOLA 4 §1.2.1 allowed `ibid` (with pinpoint `ibid 271–78.`) for the immediately preceding footnote and repeated full citations as an accepted alternative.
- **§1.2.1** — Legislation short form is declared at the end of the full citation in round brackets with single quotes and then used without `(n X)`: `Social Action, Responsibility and Heroism Act 2015 (‘SARAH’) s 1.` then `SARAH, s 2.` OSCOLA 4 §1.2.1 put the short form in round brackets without quotes (`(Working Time Directive)`).
- **§1.2.1** — Two works by one author: surname plus footnote number distinguishes them (`Williams (n 1) 240.`, `Williams (n 5) 472.`); if both were in one footnote, add the (short) title: `Ashworth, ‘Testing Fidelity to Legal Values’ (n 27) 635–37.`; `Ashworth, Positive Obligations in Criminal Law (n 27) 73.`
- **§3.1.5** — Secondary-source short forms follow §1.2.1. OSCOLA 4 §3.1 (preamble) said the same: surname + `(n n)` + pinpoint.
- **§2.1.2, §1.2.1** — Case short forms are roman for the citation but the case-name element stays italic; `v` is roman-italic as part of the name, never a separate roman `v` (see Unresolved 3 for the styling of a bare party short form).
- **§2.1.2** — A case cited in full in the text is short-formed in later footnotes; a case name given only in the text on first citation still gets the short form plus `(n X)` in later footnotes (`Austin (n 1)`).

### Secondary sources

- **§3.1.1** — Author names as printed, minus titles and postnominals; more than three authors: first name then `and others`; organisations as author when no individual; otherwise start with the title; editors treated like authors. Two or three authors joined with `and` (`K Zweigert and H Kötz`, `Mark Elliott, Jack Williams and Alison L Young`). Website and social-media authors are cited by username.
- **§3.1.2** — Italic titles for books and anything with an ISBN; every other title in single inverted commas, roman; title case with minor words lower case.
- **§3.1.3** — Pinpoints at the end: `pt`, `ch`, `para(s)`; bare page numbers; footnote pinpoint as page/para then `fn` number: `Mark Leeming, Common Law, Equity and Statute: A Complex Entangled System (Federation Press 2023) 9 fn 6.` Audio/video timestamps `14:14–18:30` (new; key changes ch 3). OSCOLA 4 §3.1.3 allowed `167ff` and a comma between chapter and page.
- **§3.2.1** — Books: author, comma, italic title, then `(additional info, edition, publisher year)`; no place of publication; edition as `2nd edn` / `rev edn`; publisher and year separated by a space only. Examples: `JG Fleming, The Law of Torts (9th edn, LBC 1998).`; `Timothy Endicott, Administrative Law (OUP 2009).`; `Leigh Sagar and Jack Burroughs, The Digital Estate (2nd edn, Sweet & Maxwell 2022).`
- **§3.2.1** — Volumes after the publication details unless details differ per volume: `WS Holdsworth, A History of English Law (Methuen & Co 1965) vol XV, 300.`; `Christian von Bar, The Common European Law of Torts, vol 2 (CH Beck 2000).` Book paragraphs take `para`, never square brackets: `Adrian Briggs, Agreements on Jurisdiction and Choice of Law (OUP 2008) para 4.51.`; pages `125–26`.
- **§3.2.2** — Ebooks without print pagination: `William Lucy, Philosophy of Private Law (Kindle edn, OUP 2007) ch 1, text to n 16.` (new section).
- **§3.2.3** — Editors/translators as authors with `(ed)`, `(eds)`, `(tr)`, `(trs)`; when there is also an author, the editor or translator opens the bracket: `K Zweigert and H Kötz, An Introduction to Comparative Law (Tony Weir tr, 3rd edn, OUP 1998).`; `HLA Hart, Punishment and Responsibility (John Gardner ed, 2nd edn, OUP 2008).`
- **§3.2.4** — Chapters: author, quoted chapter title, `in` editor `(ed),` italic book title, bracketed details; no start page; pinpoint after the bracket: `Donal Nolan and John Davies, ‘Torts and Equitable Wrongs’ in Andrew Burrows (ed), Principles of the Law of Obligations (OUP 2015) para 2.87.`
- **§3.2.5** — Older works: `Thomas Hobbes, Leviathan (first published 1651, Penguin 1985) 268.`
- **§3.2.7–3.2.8** — Encyclopedias: `Halsbury’s Laws (5th edn, 2010) vol 57, para 53.`; dictionary entries: `‘no-fault compensation’, Oxford Dictionary of Law (10th edn, OUP 2022).`
- **§3.3** — Articles: author, comma, quoted title, then `[year]` (year identifies volume) or `(year) volume`, journal abbreviation without full stops, first page; pinpoint after a comma: `Paul Craig, ‘Theory, “Pure Theory” and Values in Public Law’ [2005] PL 440, 441.`; `Alison L Young, ‘In Defence of Due Deference’ (2009) 72 MLR 554.`; `JAG Griffith, ‘The Common Law and the Political Constitution’ (2001) 117 LQR 42, 64.` Issue number only when pagination restarts per issue: `(2010) 1(1) EJLT`. Forthcoming: append `(forthcoming)`. Nested quotation marks inside an article title are double.
- **§3.4–3.5** — Case notes without a title use the italic case name and end `(note)`; book reviews end `(book review)` and an untitled review uses the book's name in quotes, roman: `AG Guest, ‘An Introduction to the Law of Contract’ (1961) 24 MLR 658 (book review).` (Book reviews new in 5th edition.)
- **§3.6** — Working papers like electronic articles: `John M Finnis, ‘On “Public Reason”’ (2006) Oxford Legal Studies Research Paper 1/2007, 8 <https://perma.cc/2DG8-LUHP>.`
- **§3.7.1** — Websites and blogs: author, quoted title, `(italic site name, date)`, link in angle brackets. Persistent link (Perma.cc) or DOI preferred; with either, no access date, and a DOI is not angle-bracketed: `Maximilian Steinbeis, ‘A European Network of Constitutional Law Blogs’ (VerfBlog, 17 March 2015) DOI: 10.17176/20181005-145902-0.`; `Cyclefree, ‘Is This Really Necessary, Minister?’ (Legal Feminist, 27 April 2023) <https://perma.cc/3THK-P4AX>.` Ordinary URL: `<https://…> accessed 21 July 2023.` No author: start with the title. Do not cite PDFs or downloads. OSCOLA 4 §3.4.8 had only the plain-URL-plus-access-date form: `Sarah Cole, ‘Virtual Friend Fires Employee’ (Naked Law, 1 May 2009) <www.nakedlaw.com/2009/05/index.html> accessed 19 November 2009`.
- **§3.7.1** — Social media (new): username as author (with `@` for X/Twitter), then `(platform, date, time and zone)` and link: `@The Criminal Bar (Twitter, 26 June 2023, 9:13pm GMT+1) <https://perma.cc/HD6K-3GZQ>.`
- **§3.7.1** — Online video: timestamp pinpoint before the link: `UK Supreme Court, ‘Lady Hale’s Valedictory Remarks – 18 December 2019’ (YouTube, 18 December 2019) 42:41–51:17 <https://perma.cc/7YZV-Y43A>.`
- **§3.7.2** — Podcasts (new): podcast name, quoted episode title, `(full date)`, timestamp, link: `Double Jeopardy podcast, ‘Episode 27: Dr Bryn Harris – Free Speech, Harm and the Internet’ (7 April 2023) 3:40–3:56 <https://…> accessed 21 July 2023.`
- **§3.7.3** — Lectures/speeches: `Amal Clooney and Philippa Webb, ‘Waging Justice in an Age of Authoritarianism’ (Lecture at St Hugh’s College, Oxford, 15 May 2023).`
- **§3.7.4** — Interviews: `Interview with Irene Kull, Assistant Dean, Faculty of Law, Tartu University (4 August 2003).` OSCOLA 4 §3.4.10 also gave the location inside the bracket (`(Tartu, Estonia, 4 August 2003)`).
- **§3.7.5** — Conference papers: `Ben McFarlane and Donal Nolan, ‘Remedying Reliance: …’ (Obligations III conference, Brisbane, July 2006).`
- **§3.7.6** — Theses: italic title, then `(type, university year)`: `Javan Herberg, Injunctive Relief for Wrongful Termination of Employment (DPhil thesis, University of Oxford 1989).` OSCOLA 4 §3.4.7 put the thesis title in single quotes, roman.
- **§3.7.7** — Newspapers: author, quoted title, italic paper, `(date)`, page: `Jane Croft, ‘Supreme Court Warns on Quality’ Financial Times (1 July 2010) 3.` Online: URL and access date instead of a page. OSCOLA 4 §3.4.9 included the city: `Financial Times (London, 1 July 2010) 3`.
- **§3.7.10** — Other government documents by general principles: `Cabinet Office, ‘Ministerial Code’ (December 2022).`
- **§3.7.12** — Personal communications: `Letter from Gordon Brown to Catherine Ashton (20 November 2009).`; `Email from … to author (16 December 2008).`
- **§3.7.13** — Generative AI (new): `ChatGPT, response to ‘Explain how artificial intelligence works’, OpenAI (16 July 2023).`

### Quotations

- **§1.5** — Single inverted commas; quotations within quotations take double. Quotations of up to three lines run in the text, introduced by at most a comma; punctuation follows the closing mark unless part of the quotation; the footnote marker comes last, after mark and punctuation.
- **§1.5** — Quotations longer than three lines are indented (no first-line indent), a line space above and below, no quotation marks; inner quotations then take single marks. Ellipsis `…` for omissions, never at the start, spaced from adjacent text. `[sic]` after errors. `(emphasis added)`, `(emphasis in original)`, `(footnote omitted)` / `(citations omitted)` go after the citation in the footnote: `[2008] UKHL 48, [2009] 1 AC 61 [12] (emphasis added).`
- **§1.5** — Quoting a work that itself cites: drop the inner footnote marker and add `citing …` in the footnote: `… (2004) 120 LQR 263, 276, citing Clingham and McCann [2002] UKHL 39, [2003] 1 AC 787 [83] (Lord Hope).` OSCOLA 4 §1.5 is materially the same (three-line threshold, single outer marks) but placed "emphasis added" comments in the footnote without specifying position.

### Bibliography and tables

- **§1.6** — Long works carry a list of abbreviations then tables (cases, then legislation, then other tables) in the preliminaries; primary sources live in the tables, not the bibliography.
- **§1.6.2** — Table of cases: names not italicised; split by jurisdiction unless few cases; alphabetical by first significant word (`Farquar’s Estate, Re`; `F (mental patient: sterilisation), Re`); `R v Smith` listed as `Smith` in criminal-law works, otherwise under `R`; ship and trade-mark cases get a cross-reference entry (`Starsin, The. See Homburg Houtimport BV v Agrosin Private Ltd`); EU cases alphabetical with the case number in round brackets then the ECLI: `Schempp v Finanzamt (Case C-403/03) EU:C:2005:446`; entries indexed to pages: `A v Bottrill [2002] UKPC 44, [2003] 1 AC 449 … 163`. OSCOLA 4 §1.6.2 tabled EU cases with the ECR reference and optionally in a separate chronological table.
- **§1.6.3** — Table of legislation after the cases; alphabetical by first significant word; each Act sub-divided by provision (`s 1`, `sch 1B, art 29`, `s 47C(1)`); SIs listed separately after statutes; indexed.
- **§1.7** — Bibliography (secondary sources only) after the text and appendices, not indexed. Entries as in footnotes except: surname first, then initials with no comma between and a comma after the initials; initials not forenames; unattributed works first, preceded by a double em dash; no full stop at the end; no division by source type. Example: `Fisher E, Risk Regulation and Administrative Constitutionalism (Hart Publishing 2007)`
- **§1.7** — Same author: chronological, then alphabetical within a year, name replaced by `——` after the first entry; co-authored works after sole works, alphabetical by co-author, co-author repeated each time: `Hart HLA, Law, Liberty and Morality (OUP 1963)` / `—— ‘Varieties of Responsibility’ (1967) 83 LQR 346` / `—— and Honoré AM, Causation in the Law (OUP 1959)`. OSCOLA 4 §1.7 is identical apart from an explicit statement that it need not be divided by type (5th edition adds this).

### Parliamentary and international

- **§3.7.8** — Hansard: `HC Deb 3 February 1977, vol 389, cols 973–76.`; `HL Deb 12 November 2009, vol 714, col 893.`; Public Bill committee `Health Bill Deb 30 January 2007, cols 12–15.`; old standing committees `SC Deb (A) 13 May 1998, col 345.` OSCOLA 4 §3.4.2 also gave written-answer forms (`col 505W`, `col WA261`) and the `PBC Deb (Bill 99)` alternative; the 5th edition omits both (Unresolved 4).
- **§3.7.8** — Select committee reports: committee, italic title, `(HL/HC session, paper number–volume)`: `Health Committee, Patient Safety (HC 2008–2009, 151–I) paras 173–75.`; joint committees `(2009–10, HL 73, HC 425)`.
- **§3.7.9** — Command papers: body, italic title, `(descriptor, number, year)`; prefixes `C` (1833–99), `Cd` (1900–18), `Cmd` (1919–56), `Cmnd` (1957–86), `Cm` (1986–2018), `CP` (2019–): `Department for Science, Innovation & Technology, A Pro-innovation Approach to AI Regulation (CP 815, 2023) paras 23–25.` OSCOLA 4 §3.4.3 stopped at `Cm`.

## NZLSG 3

### Footnote mechanics

- **§2.1.2** — Full citation only in footnotes; main text uses the case name or work title. First footnote gives the full citation even when the name is in the text.
- **§2.2.4(a)** — Footnotes begin with a capital and end with a full stop; several sources are separated by semicolons with `and` before the last: `Edwards v O’Connor [1991] 2 NZLR 543 (CA); L G Thorne & Co v Thomas Borthwick & Sons [1956] SR (NSW) 81 (SC); and State Rail Authority v Heath Outdoor Ltd (1986) 7 NSWLR 170 (CA).`
- **§2.2.4(b)** — Signals `See`, `See also`, `See generally`, `Compare`, `But see`, `Contrast`, `… and following`; no `cf`, `ff`, `et seq`.
- **§1.1.2** — Punctuation only when grammatical; no full stops in abbreviations (`Kós P`, `CA`); no serial comma unless needed for clarity.
- **§1.1.3** — Italicise party names including the `v`, newspaper names, book titles and titles of government reports; Māori words are not italicised.
- **§1.2.3(a)/(g)** — Ranges use en dashes and full digits: `at [32]–[38]`, `360–365`; dates `28 October 1965`.

### Cases

- **§3.1** — Precedence: official neutral citation always when one exists (unreported: neutral citation alone; reported: neutral citation then the single best report, NZLR first, then unofficial series); no neutral citation: best report; neither: file-number form. Examples: `North Shore City Council v Attorney-General [2010] NZSC 125.`; `AstraZeneca Ltd v Commerce Commission [2009] NZSC 92, [2010] 1 NZLR 297 at [29].`; `Hawkins v Minister of Justice [1991] 2 NZLR 530 (CA) at 534.`; `Shell New Zealand Ltd v Porirua City Council CA57/05, 19 May 2005 at [5].`
- **§3.2 (form)** — Reported: name | neutral citation, | [year] or (year) | volume | series | first page | (court, only without a neutral citation) | `at` pinpoint. `Z v Dental Complaints Assessment Committee [2008] NZSC 55, [2009] 1 NZLR 1 at [26].`; `Taylor v New Zealand Poultry Board [1984] 1 NZLR 394 (CA) at 398.`
- **§3.2.1(a)–(b)** — Case name italic including `v`; parties as on the report's first page minus full stops; no given names or initials for natural persons; first plaintiff and first defendant only (no `& Ors`); status abbreviations `Ltd`, `Inc`, `Co`, `Corp`, `Pty`, `plc`, `(in liq)`, `(in rec)`, `(in stat man)`; do not abbreviate `Attorney-General`, `Commissioner of Inland Revenue`, `City Council`.
- **§3.2.1(c)–(d)** — The Crown is `R` in unreported cases and cited as printed in reported ones (`Thomas v The Queen [1972] NZLR 34 (CA).`); appellant first regardless of the Crown's role. `Re` replaces "In re"; `ex parte` is not abbreviated: `Re Paterson, ex parte Kingston [1997] 1 NZLR 371 (HC).`
- **§3.2.1(e)–(f)** — Common or abbreviated names go in square brackets after the footnote citation (round brackets, italic, in text) and are used on later references: `Simpson v Attorney-General [1994] 3 NZLR 667 (CA) [Baigent’s case].`
- **§3.2.1(g)–(i)** — Suppressed names as designated (`T v H`; `R v E (CA380/06) [2008] NZCA 404, [2008] 3 NZLR 145.`); numbered sequels `(No 2)`; consolidated cases cite the first name only.
- **§3.2.2** — Neutral citation inserted after the name when official; NZLII pseudo-neutral citations are never used; comma between neutral citation and report.
- **§3.2.3–3.2.4** — Square-bracket year when the series is organised by year (year of report), round when by volume (year of judgment); volume number only when needed; Arabic numerals. NZLR by volume until 1916 (`(1887) 5 NZLR CA 111.`), by year from 1916, with volume numbers from 1973. With a neutral citation and a volume-organised report both years appear: `Trustees Executors Ltd v QBE Insurance (International) Ltd [2011] NZCA 53, (2011) 20 PRNZ 253 at [8].`
- **§3.2.5** — Series abbreviated without punctuation; official series preferred (NZLR over AC for Privy Council appeals from NZ: `Buchanan v Jennings [2004] UKPC 36, [2005] 2 NZLR 577.`); general series over subject series; series number in round brackets for foreign multi-series reports `(1999) 174 DLR (4th) 45 (SCC)`.
- **§3.2.6** — Starting page bare (no `p`); case-number series keep their form (`[2001] 2 Cr App R 3`; CCH `¶41-817`).
- **§3.2.7** — Court identifier in round brackets after the starting page only when there is no neutral citation (`(CA)`, `(HC)`, `(SC)` for the pre-1980 Supreme Court, `(FC)`, `(PC)`); never after a neutral citation. Omitted for single-court series (CLR, US, SCR). Jurisdiction added only in multi-jurisdiction series: `R v B [1996] 1 LRC 517 (NZCA).`
- **§3.2.8** — Pinpoints follow the court identifier, always prefixed by `at`; paragraphs in square brackets with no `para` (`at [29]`, `at [238(a)]`); pages bare (`at 398`); repeat the first page when pinpointing to it (`at 168`); several pinpoints comma separated with `and` before the last (`at [30], [40] and [47]`); ranges with an unspaced en dash and full digits (`at 92–98`, `at [153]–[162]`).
- **§3.2.9** — Judge after the pinpoint with `per`: `Mafart v Television New Zealand Ltd [2006] NZSC 33, [2006] 3 NZLR 18 at [48] per Tipping J.`; `… at [6] per Elias CJ dissenting.`; offices `CJ`, `P`, `J`, `JJ`, and `Associate Judge`, `Master`, `Judge` written before the name.
- **§3.2.10–3.2.11** — No parallel citations beyond neutral citation plus best report. History with `aff’d` / `rev’d` after a semicolon: `Foodstuffs (Auckland) Ltd v Commerce Commission [2002] 1 NZLR 353 (CA); rev’d [2002] UKPC 25, [2004] 1 NZLR 145.`
- **§3.3** — Neutral citation: name | [year] | court identifier | judgment number | `at` pinpoint: `Attorney-General v X [2007] NZCA 388 at [70].` Adoption years: NZSC 2005 (2004 judgments have none), UKPC 2001, NZCA 2007, NZHC 2012, NZDC 2015, NZACC 2010, NZEmpC 2010, NZEnvC 2010, NZFC 2012, NZYC 2013; tribunals from about 2010 (NZHRRT from August 2007). District-based tribunals put the district before the number: `Griffin v Citibus Ltd [2011] NZERA Christchurch 137`; `Smith v Jones [2010] NZTT Wellington 904`.
- **§3.4** — Unreported without neutral citation: name | court abbreviation and registry (multi-registry courts only) | file number, | date | `at` pinpoint: `R v Reekie CA339/03, 3 August 2004 at [35].`; `R v Tuhou HC Napier CRI-2007-020-2820, 11 September 2008 at [13].`; `KAB v PRJ FC Tauranga FAM-2008-070-1800, 17 February 2009.`; `Chirnside v Fay SC CIV 7/2004, 26 August 2004.` File numbers hyphenated, leading zeros dropped, first number only, comma before the date; month in full. Minutes: `(Minute No 16)` after the date.
- **§3.5** — Māori Land Court: name – block name (en dash) | (year) | volume | minute book title `MB` | folio | (abbreviated citation): `Pacey v Adlam – Matata Parish 39A 2B 2B 2A (2017) 178 Waiariki MB 32 (178 WAR 32).` Uncontested: applicant only (`Hutchison – Torere 64 Incorporation (2014) 109 Waiariki MB 260 (109 WAR 260).`); succession `Martin – Estate of Jackie Te Ratu Tio (2013) 305 Aotea MB 1 (305 AOT 1).` Minute books: TTK, WMN, WAR, TRW, TKT, AOT, TWP, APPEAL, CJ.
- **§3.5.3–3.5.5** — Māori Appellate Court from 2010 and the Chief Judge's minute book use square-bracket years and the year in place of a volume: `Faulkner v Hoete – Motiti North C No 1 [2018] Māori Appellate Court MB 17 (2018 APPEAL 17).`; `McCallum v The Māori Trustee – Estate of Ngapiki Waaka Hakaraia [2017] Chief Judge’s MB 144 (2017 CJ 144).` Pre-2010 appellate: `Craig v Kira – Wainui 2F4D (2006) 7 Taitokerau Appellate MB 1 (7 APWH 1).` Pinpoint per §3.2.8.
- **§3.6** — Waitangi Tribunal: `Waitangi Tribunal` (author, no comma) | italic title | `(Wai number, year)` | `at` pinpoint: `Waitangi Tribunal The East Coast Settlement Report (Wai 2190, 2010) at 51.`; `Waitangi Tribunal Ko Aotearoa Tēnei: … (Wai 262, 2011) vol 2 at 500.`
- **§3.7–3.8** — Historic newspaper-reported judgments: `Jones v Smith SC Wellington, 2 April 1844 reported in The New Zealand Gazette and Wellington Spectator (Wellington, 17 April 1844) 3 at 3.` Supreme Court transcripts: `Westpac New Zealand Ltd v Map & Associates Ltd [2011] NZSC Trans 15 at 7.`; pre-2011 `Dollars & Sense Ltd v Nathan Transcript SC31/2007, 22 November 2007.`
- **§7.1.9** — Cases found online keep the normal case form; proprietary database citations (`2006 WL …`) are avoided.

### Legislation

- **§4.1.1(a)–(c)** — Short title exactly as enacted, roman (never italic), no full stops; year of enactment; no `(NZ)` unless needed to distinguish; foreign Acts carry the jurisdiction after the year: `Counter-Terrorism Act 2008 (UK), s 92.`; `Trusts (Special Provisions) Act 1989 (Bermuda).`
- **§4.1.1(d)** — Pinpoint after the year with a comma (`Crimes Act 1961, s 59.`) or in text before the title (`s 36 of the Commerce Act 1986`). Labels: `s/ss`, `subs/subss`, `para/paras`, `sch/schs`, `pt/pts`, `art/arts`, `r/rr`, `sub-r`, `reg/regs`, `sub-reg`, `cl/cls`, `sub-cl`. A subsection within its section is cited as a section (`s 67(2)`, `s 2(a)(ii)`); schedule provisions have no comma or part: `Property Law Act 2007, sch 3 cl 4.`; definitions `s 2 definition of “family chattels”, para (b)`.
- **§4.1.1(e)** — `Te Tiriti o Waitangi 1840, art 3.` or `Treaty of Waitangi 1840, art 3.`
- **§4.1.3–4.1.4** — Pre-1854 ordinances add regnal year and number (`Distillation Prohibition Ordinance 1841 4 Vict 5, cl 1.`); provincial Acts add the province (`Nelson Waterworks Act 1863 (Nelson).`); alphanumeric sections as printed (`Income Tax Act 2004, s CE 10.`).
- **§4.2.1** — Bills: short title | year of introduction | (bill number-bar number) | `, cl`: `Judicial Matters Bill 2008 (216-1), cl 3.`; bar number optional when the version is immaterial (`Judicial Retirement Age Bill 2006 (90).`); numbered bills `Arms Amendment Bill (No 3) 2005 (248-1).`; divided bills `(180-3A)`.
- **§4.2.2–4.2.3** — Explanatory notes and select committee reports on bills: `Judicial Matters Bill 2008 (216-1) (explanatory note) at 5.`; `Unit Titles Bill 2008 (212-2) (select committee report) at 4.`; SOPs `Supplementary Order Paper 2006 (79) Evidence Bill 2005 (256-1) (explanatory note) at 3.`
- **§4.3.1** — Legislative instruments: title | year | `, reg`/`r`/`cl`: `Costs in Criminal Cases Regulations 1987, reg 3.`; `Lotto Amendment Rules 2010, r 6.`; `Minimum Wage Order 2010, cl 4(a).`
- **§4.3.2–4.3.6** — Gazette-published instruments: `“Royal Commission on the Pike River Coal Mine Tragedy” (16 December 2010) 173 New Zealand Gazette 4261 at 4262.`; court rules `High Court Rules 2016, r 14.3.`; other instruments `Civil Aviation Rules, r 19.5.`; letters patent `… 1983, cl 12.`

### Subsequent references

- **§2.1.1, §2.3.1** — First citation in full; later citations use the cross-referencing style, never `ibid`. Two styles: general (student, academic, court submissions, judgments) and commercial (NZ commercial publishers).
- **§2.3.1(a)** — General style rule 1: if the source is obvious from context, give only the pinpoint, capitalised as a footnote (`At 535.`; `At [52] per Tipping J.`; legislation `Section 8.`). Rule 2: otherwise repeat the identifier and write `, above n x,` then the pinpoint.
- **§2.3.1(a)(i)** — Cases: `R v Wang, above n 49, at 533.`; with no pinpoint `Rainy Sky SA v Kookmin Bank, above n 10.` Reference tag optional (§2.3.2): `Baigent’s case, above n 4, at 668.`; `Westdeutsche, above n 5, at 698.` Tags keep the original formatting (italic case tags).
- **§2.3.1(a)(ii)** — Legislation: section only when obvious; otherwise short title without year or jurisdiction unless needed to disambiguate: `Securities Act, s 63.` but `Companies Act 1955, s 185.` / `Companies Act 1993, s 185.`; no `above n` for legislation.
- **§2.3.1(a)(iii)** — Texts and articles: author surname(s) then `, above n x, at …`: `Spiller, above n 21, at 70.`; `Londono, Eady and Smith, above n 23, at [13-10].` Add the title when one footnote holds several works by the author or same-surname authors: `Smith “Rethinking the Defence of Mistake”, above n 25, at 431.`; `Robertson The Justice Game, above n 26, at 20.`
- **§2.3.1(a)(iv)** — Chapters follow the text rule (`Mullan, above n 40, at 152.`); a different chapter from the same edited book is cited in full again.
- **§2.3.1(a)(v)** — Multi-source footnote followed by a citation to one of them uses `, above n x,` (`Glazebrook, above n 1, at 249.`) unless the pinpoint alone identifies it (`Section 62.`).
- **§2.3.1(b)** — Commercial style: cases and legislation repeated in full on every citation, no reference tags; other sources follow the general style.
- **§1.1.7(c)** — Reference tags introduced in round brackets in text and square brackets in footnotes, separately for each.

### Quotations

- **§1.2.2(a)(i)** — Short quotations (fewer than 30 words) run in the text in double quotation marks; closing mark after quoted punctuation; sentence-ending punctuation inside only when the whole sentence is quoted; footnote number at the end of the sentence after all punctuation (directly after the quotation only when the sentence has more than one footnote).
- **§1.2.2(a)(ii)** — Long quotations (30 words or more) are indented both sides in a smaller font, no quotation marks, introduced by a colon with the footnote number immediately after the colon, blank line above and below; a quotation starting at a numbered paragraph of a judgment keeps the `[41]` and the footnote then omits the pinpoint.
- **§1.2.2(b)** — Nested: a short quotation inside a short quotation takes single marks; a short quotation inside a long (unmarked) quotation takes double marks; a long quotation inside a long quotation is indented further, no marks. Ellipsis `…` (the symbol, spaced) for omissions; original punctuation retained.
- **§2.2.2** — Quotation footnotes: the marker for a short quotation goes at sentence end; for a long quotation after the introducing colon.

## Unresolved (DECISION-040 candidates)

1. **OSCOLA 5 page pinpoint without a closing bracket.** §2.1.6 states the no-comma rule only for citations ending in a bracketed court identifier and gives no example of a page pinpoint following a citation whose last element is a report page (MNC + report, no paragraph numbers). OSCOLA 4 §2.1.6 required a comma there. Whether the 5th edition silently kept or dropped that comma is unconfirmed.
2. **OSCOLA 5 subsequent legislation citation with no declared short form.** §1.2.1 shows only the declared-short-form route (`SARAH, s 2.`). It does not say whether an undeclared Act is repeated in full or short-formed with `(n X)`. OSCOLA 4 §1.2.1 said a further full citation is given.
3. **OSCOLA 5 styling of a bare party short form.** `Phelps (n 14)` and `Austin (n 1) [34]` are shown with the party name in italic in the PDF layout, but pdftotext discards styling; the examples cannot confirm whether a single-party short form is italic. The quick reference gives no short-form example.
4. **OSCOLA 5 Hansard written answers and `PBC Deb (Bill n)`.** OSCOLA 4 §3.4.2 gave `col 505W`, `col WA261` and the `PBC Deb (Bill 99)` alternative; §3.7.8 of the 5th edition omits them without saying they are withdrawn.
5. **OSCOLA 5 session ranges in select committee citations.** §3.7.8 examples show `(HL 2008–2009, 107–I)` and `(HC 2008–2009, 151–I)` while §1.3.2 prescribes `1925–27`-style year ranges and the joint committee example uses `(2009–10, …)`. Which form the fixtures should expect is unconfirmed.
6. **OSCOLA 5 platform name for X.** §3.7.1's example labels an X post `(Twitter, …)` and the text refers to "X (previously known as Twitter)"; whether posts made after the rename should carry `X` is not stated.
7. **OSCOLA 5 appendix abbreviations (§5.2) not extracted.** Journal, report and case-name abbreviation lists were not fetched; fixtures should use abbreviations that appear in the body examples only.
8. **NZLSG online video (non-podcast) and non-Twitter social media.** §7.1.8 covers podcasts including video podcasts and §7.1.10 covers Twitter only; there is no rule for YouTube-style video or other platforms (the general §7.1.1 form is the only candidate).
9. **NZLSG bibliography placement of Waitangi Tribunal and Māori Land Court material.** Appendix 7 lists cases, legislation, treaties, books, articles, parliamentary/government materials, reports, dissertations and Internet resources; it does not say whether Waitangi Tribunal reports go under cases or reports.
10. **NZLSG bibliography author order versus the sample.** Appendix 7 says to order secondary sources by the first author's surname while printing names uninverted; confirmed by the sample (`Butler`, `Chambers`, `Finn`, `Harris`), but the rule for institutional authors beginning with "New Zealand" (drop "the" only) was not exemplified.
11. **NZLSG commercial-style pinpoints in repeated case citations.** §2.3.1(b) requires the full citation each time; it does not show whether the `at` pinpoint of the current footnote replaces or follows the first-citation pinpoint (assumed: the current pinpoint only).
12. **NZLSG chapters 8 and 9 (foreign cases and legislation) not fetched.** UK/Australian case forms under NZLSG were confirmed only from incidental examples (`Rainy Sky SA v Kookmin Bank [2011] UKSC 50, [2011] 1 WLR 2900`; `Cowan v Scargill [1985] Ch 270 (Ch)` in Appendix 7, which uses `(Ch)` and `(HL)` as UK court identifiers); §8.4 and §9.4 should be read before fixtures for foreign sources under NZLSG are written.
13. **NZLSG online commentaries, looseleafs and encyclopaedias (§6.3, §6.5, §6.6)** and OSCOLA 5 `§4.2.4–4.2.6`, `§4.3.2–4.3.4`, `§4.3.6`, `§4.5` were not read in full; no fixture should assert forms for those source types until they are.
14. **Quotation-mark style inside titles under NZLSG.** §6.4.3 and §7.1.3 say quotation marks within a title are single; whether a title quoted inside an italic book title (no outer marks) keeps double marks is not addressed.

## Resolved by this epic (DECISION-040 part (a))

Confirmed from the guide texts above and now implemented; the remaining points of the Unresolved list are DECISION-040 part (b) in `docs/decisions.md`.

1. AGLC4 r 1.5.1 — block quotations begin at four lines (three lines or fewer stay inline); a quotation within a short quotation takes double marks, within a long (unmarked) quotation single marks. The quotation tools previously switched at three lines and did not alternate the inner marks.
2. OSCOLA 5 §1.2.1, §1.2.3 — `ibid` is not used; every later reference is the identifier plus `(n X)` and the pinpoint. OSCOLA 4 keeps lower-case `ibid`.
3. OSCOLA 5 §2.1.6 — a paragraph pinpoint after a neutral citation (or the report that follows it) takes no comma: `[2008] 1 AC 884 [42]`.
4. OSCOLA 5 §2.1.1 — the case name is italic including the `v`.
5. OSCOLA §1.5 (both editions) — single quotation marks, double within.
6. NZLSG 3 §1.2.2(a)–(b) — block quotations from 30 words; double quotation marks, single within.
7. NZLSG 3 §2.3.1(a) — general style: the pinpoint alone, capitalised, when the source is obvious (`At 535.`); otherwise identifier, `, above n x,` and the pinpoint; legislation by short title alone; no `ibid`.
8. NZLSG 3 Appendix 7 — bibliography names are not inverted; entries follow the footnote form minus pinpoints and end with a full stop.
9. Court mode applies to AGLC standards only (engine invariant, STD-013): the court toggles are ignored under OSCOLA and NZLSG, and Settings offers the writing mode only under an AGLC standard.
