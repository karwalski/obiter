OSCOLA 5 and OSCOLA 4

UK legal citation in Microsoft Word: footnotes, tables of cases and legislation, and a bibliography in the OSCOLA form.

OSCOLA in Obiter

The Oxford University Standard for the Citation of Legal Authorities (OSCOLA) is the citation style of United Kingdom legal writing. Its fifth edition, OSCOLA 5, was published by the Faculty of Law, University of Oxford, in 2026. Obiter implements OSCOLA 5 and the 2012 fourth edition, OSCOLA 4, alongside the AGLC.

Choose the standard from the Citation standard control in Settings. The choice is stored in the document, so one document can use OSCOLA while another uses AGLC4 or NZLSG, and the whole document is re-rendered on the next refresh. The Insert and Edit forms show the fields OSCOLA reads (a UK case takes its neutral citation year, court and number), and Record details links UK cases to BAILII.

What Obiter formats under OSCOLA 5

Rule numbers below are OSCOLA 5’s own.

Cases

The case name in italics, including the v; the neutral citation, a comma and the best report (rr 2.1.1, 2.1.3); a court identifier only where there is no neutral citation (r 2.1.5). Paragraph pinpoints follow in square brackets with no comma, and page pinpoints follow a bracketed court identifier without a comma (r 2.1.6). Scottish (r 2.2.1), Northern Irish (r 2.3.1) and Irish cases take their own forms.

Legislation

Short title and year in roman, with the provision after a comma, such as Human Rights Act 1998, s 2 (rr 2.4.1, 2.4.2); statutory instruments with their SI number (r 2.5.1); Welsh, Scottish and Northern Irish legislation (rr 2.4.6–2.4.8); and assimilated EU law (r 2.4.9). A short form declared on the first citation is used alone after that (r 1.2.1).

Subsequent references without ibid

OSCOLA 5 removed ibid (rr 1.2.1, 1.2.3). A later reference gives the short identifier, the footnote of the full citation and the pinpoint, such as Austin (n 1) [34] or Stevens (n 1) 110. Obiter keeps every footnote number right as the document changes.

Quotations

Single quotation marks, with double marks inside; quotations longer than three lines set as an indented block without marks (r 1.5). The Quote panel applies these when the document uses OSCOLA.

Secondary sources

Books with the edition, publisher and year (r 3.2.1); chapters (r 3.2.4); articles with the title in single quotes (r 3.3); theses with an italic title (r 3.7.6); websites, blogs, social media, podcasts and online video (rr 3.7.1, 3.7.2); newspapers (r 3.7.7); Hansard (r 3.7.8); command papers (r 3.7.9); Law Commission reports (r 3.7.11); and generative AI output in the form OSCOLA 5 sets (r 3.7.13).

UK, EU and ECHR materials

Treaties (r 4.1.1); UN documents and resolutions (r 4.2); the International Court of Justice (r 4.3); EU legislation (r 4.4.1); Court of Justice and General Court cases by ECLI (r 4.4.2); and the European Court and Commission of Human Rights (rr 4.4.4, 4.4.5).

Tables and bibliography

The Bibliography view generates a Table of Cases with case names in roman (r 1.6.2), a Table of Legislation (r 1.6.3) and a Bibliography of secondary sources, surname first and with no closing full stop (r 1.7).

Validation

The OSCOLA checks run, including ibid, missing neutral citations and table structure, and the AGLC-only typography checks stay silent. A reported case is expected to carry both its neutral citation and its best report.

OSCOLA 4

Choose OSCOLA 4 when a publisher, journal or course still requires the 2012 edition. Obiter applies the points where the fourth edition differs:

  • Ibid for the immediately preceding footnote (r 1.2.1 of that edition), and the ibid check is not raised.
  • Declared short forms without quotation marks.
  • Thesis titles in single quotes (r 3.4.7) and an access date after a URL (r 3.4.8).

Everything else uses the same OSCOLA forms, tables and bibliography.

How OSCOLA support is checked

OSCOLA 5 and OSCOLA 4 were reviewed against the published guides in September 2026. Rule notes derived from the guides drive a feature-matrix test suite that runs alongside the core AGLC4 engine, and a coverage test fails if any source type lacks a declared treatment under OSCOLA.

Where OSCOLA has no form of its own for a source type, such as an Australian-only source, Obiter uses the AGLC4 form. A small number of secondary source types, such as speeches, press releases and dictionaries, still render in their AGLC4 form while their OSCOLA forms are built. The standards coverage table lists every source type, and points the guide leaves open are recorded for researchers rather than guessed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Obiter support OSCOLA 5?

Yes. Obiter implements OSCOLA 5, the 2026 edition of the Oxford University Standard for the Citation of Legal Authorities, and OSCOLA 4 for publishers that still require it. Choose the standard per document in Settings.

Does OSCOLA 5 use ibid?

No. OSCOLA 5 removed ibid. A later reference gives a short identifier, the footnote of the full citation and the pinpoint, such as Austin (n 1) [34]. Obiter writes these on every refresh. OSCOLA 4 still allows ibid for the immediately preceding footnote, and Obiter applies it when the document uses OSCOLA 4.

Can Obiter produce an OSCOLA table of cases?

Yes. Under OSCOLA the Bibliography view generates a Table of Cases, a Table of Legislation and a Bibliography of secondary sources, each in the OSCOLA form.
