Court mode

Court submission mode for Australian courts and tribunals: the citation form, parallel citations and List of Authorities each court asks for, inside Microsoft Word.

What court mode does

Court mode is a writing mode for submissions, outlines of argument and other documents filed in Australian courts and tribunals. It builds on the AGLC (the Australian Guide to Legal Citation, 4th edition, AGLC4) and changes what courts treat differently from academic writing. Turn it on in Settings under Writing mode, then choose the court or tribunal.

  • No ibid. Later references give the short case name or short title with the pinpoint, so each footnote identifies its source. Refresh All rewrites any existing ibid.
  • No (n X) cross-references. Court short references drop the footnote cross-reference. It has its own control, so it can be turned back on for a document.
  • Parallel citations. A reported case is cited by its authorised report and its medium neutral citation (MNC), in the order the court uses, or by the report alone where the court says the report replaces the MNC.
  • List of Authorities. The Bibliography view generates the court’s List of Authorities in place of a bibliography.
  • What you see is what you file. The Insert form, the library previews, the Quote panel and the footnotes all render the court form.

Court mode is part of the AGLC4 standard, so the Writing mode control appears while a document uses AGLC4. A document switched to OSCOLA or NZLSG returns to academic mode.

“Preferred” follows instruments that ask for the parallel citation “as far as possible”. Where a court’s instrument is silent on the order of the report and the MNC, the preset follows the court’s own example, or the order seen in its published judgments. Where no instrument was found, the preset uses the AGLC4 form and says so. A pinpoint to a report keeps the report’s starting page (AGLC4 r 2.2.5).

The full profile for each court, with the source, clause, check date and known gaps for every value, is in the court profiles reference on GitHub.

Court settings you can adjust

A preset fills in the court settings. Each one can be changed for the document under Court toggles in Settings.

  • Parallel citations — off, preferred or mandatory, and whether the report’s MNC is given with it or the report replaces it.
  • Parallel citation order — the authorised report first, or the MNC first (the Federal Court, Tasmanian and WA presets put the MNC first, as their instruments show).
  • Pinpoint style and connector — page only, paragraph only, or paragraph and page, with AGLC4 punctuation or “at” before the pinpoint (the Federal Court and Tasmanian presets use “at”).
  • Authorised-report hierarchy — the court’s preferred report series in order, used to rank a case’s reports.
  • Ibid suppression and (n X) cross-reference suppression — separate controls, both on in every preset.
  • Subsequent references to cases — the short title and pinpoint, or the case name (the WA preset, following its practice direction).
  • Unreported-judgment gate — off or warn. Warn flags an MNC cited for a case that has an authorised report.
  • List of Authorities — the layout the Bibliography view generates.

Lists of Authorities

Each layout follows the court instrument it is named after:

High Court: Joint Book of Authorities

Parts A to E under HCA Practice Direction No 2 of 2024: principal legislation, other legislation, cases in the CLR, cases in other report series, and other materials, with the version of the legislation. You mark principal legislation for Part A in Edit Citation.

Federal Court: a single list

The Lists of Authorities and Citations Practice Note (GPN-AUTH), reissued 7 May 2025, has no Part A and Part B. Obiter gives authorities, legislation, and bills and explanatory material, each in alphabetical order as in the eBooks Practice Note, with the version of the legislation in force.

NSW Court of Appeal: four categories

Legislation with the date or version to be applied, cases from which passages will be read, cases cited but not read, and secondary sources (Practice Note SC CA 1).

Victoria, Queensland, SA and family appeals: parts

Part A, B and C for the Victorian Court of Appeal, with “None” under an unused part; Part A and Part B for the Queensland Court of Appeal; and two parts, expected to be read and not expected to be read, for the SA courts and Federal Circuit and Family Court appeals.

Tasmania: three parts

Authorities counsel intends to cite, authorities that might be referred to, and legislation with its sections (Tas SC Practice Direction 3 of 2022).

WA: cases to be read marked with an asterisk

Cases and legislation listed separately and alphabetically, cases counsel intends to read from marked with an asterisk and the pages or paragraphs to be read, and a statement when no case will be read (WA Consolidated Practice Directions, PD 2.1).

Edit Citation and the Library show only the List of Authorities controls the document’s layout uses, labelled in that layout’s terms: which part an authority goes in, key authorities, the passages to be read, principal legislation, and the legislation version (“as at” a date, or “as commenced on” a date). The legislation version appears only in the court lists that ask for it, never in AGLC4 footnotes.

Court profiles travel with the document

  • Stored in the document. When you choose a court, the document keeps the full set of values and the profile version, so it renders the same way on any computer.
  • Sources in Settings. For each value, Settings shows whether it comes from the profile or was changed for the document, the kind of source (a court instrument, observed practice, or Obiter’s default where the instrument is silent), a link to the source and a plain-English note.
  • Nothing changes without asking. When a profile is corrected or a court reissues its practice note, existing documents keep their values. Settings offers Update court profile, lists each change, and applies nothing until you accept it.

Practice-direction checks and reminders

In court mode, Validation checks the document against the court’s requirements and labels each issue with the practice direction it comes from, not an AGLC rule. It flags a missing parallel citation where the preset makes it mandatory, any ibid or (n X) left in a footnote, and, with the unreported-judgment gate on, an MNC cited where an authorised report exists. For the Queensland and Tasmanian courts, the Insert form records whether a later judgment has doubted, or not followed, each cited case, and Validation notes any case without that answer, as their practice directions require.

Where a court or tribunal has issued a practice note or direction on generative AI, court mode shows a reminder with the instrument’s name, date and a link. Sixteen of the 22 presets carry one, including the Federal Court (GPN-AI), the Federal Circuit and Family Court, the NSW and Victorian Supreme Courts, the Queensland and SA courts, the WA Supreme Court and the Administrative Review Tribunal. The reminders are practice-direction guidance, not citation rules: verify that every cited authority exists and supports the point, disclose AI use where the court requires it, and do not rely on one AI tool to check another’s output.

Track Changes and handover

  • Track Changes respected. While Track Changes is on (where Word can report it), Obiter asks before it refreshes the document’s citations, and explains that the refresh will appear as tracked revisions. You can refresh anyway or skip for now.
  • Prepare for handover. One check before a document leaves you: validation issues, footnotes the last refresh left alone because they were edited by hand or locked, and Obiter’s own document properties, each with its own Remove button. Comments and tracked changes are counted, never removed.
  • No personal data in the file. Obiter writes only its version, the document’s citation standard and the date it first managed the document. It never writes a person’s name, and a property you remove stays removed.

Checked against the courts’ instruments, not endorsed

Each court profile was checked against the court’s own practice notes, practice directions or rules, and the add-in names the instrument and the date of the check (6 October 2026 for the current profiles). No court has endorsed Obiter. In the add-in each profile carries the label “Experimental: checked against [instrument] on [date]; not endorsed by the court”, because no profile yet has both a documented rule and current examples of the same kind of document to test against. Profiles are re-checked each quarter and whenever a court’s instrument changes or moves.

Some courts publish no citation instrument. For the NSW District and Local Courts, the Victorian County and Magistrates’ Courts and the tribunals, the preset uses the AGLC4 form and says that no instrument was found. Parts of some instruments are not yet modelled, such as record locators and the High Court volume index; the court profiles reference lists each gap.

Obiter output is checked for compatibility with each court’s published practice notes; it is not guaranteed to be accepted for filing. Always check the court’s current practice directions before you file.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which courts does Obiter court mode support?

Court mode has 22 presets: the High Court, Federal Court and Federal Circuit and Family Court; the NSW, Victorian and Queensland appellate, supreme and lower courts; the Supreme Courts of WA, SA, Tasmania, the ACT and the NT, and the SA District and Magistrates Courts (civil); and the Administrative Review Tribunal, the Fair Work Commission and the state and territory tribunals.

Can Obiter generate a List of Authorities?

Yes. In court mode the Bibliography view generates a List of Authorities in the layout the court’s instrument describes, including the High Court Joint Book of Authorities (Parts A to E), the Federal Court list under GPN-AUTH, the NSW Court of Appeal four-category list, the Victorian Court of Appeal Part A, B and C list, the Tasmanian three-part list and the WA list with cases to be read marked with an asterisk.

Is Obiter court mode endorsed by the courts?

No. Each court profile is checked against the court’s own published practice notes and rules, and Obiter names the instrument and the date it was checked, but no court has endorsed Obiter. Always check the court’s current practice directions before you file.
