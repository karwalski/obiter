ObiterAGLC referencing for Microsoft Word, applied automatically.

A free, open-source Microsoft Word add-in that applies the Australian Guide to Legal Citation — the AGLC — so you can focus on your argument. Obiter covers AGLC4 in full and is ready for AGLC5. Insert footnotes, generate bibliographies, and validate references — all without leaving your document.

From obiter dictum — a remark in passing. Citations are the remarks that support the argument. Also sounds like “oh, better” — which is what your citations become.

Why does Obiter exist?

AGLC4 was drafted without any model of its software implementation. The result is years of workarounds, broken tools, and unmet needs.

The official AGLC4 style hasn’t been updated since 2020

  • The Zotero CSL style was deliberately scoped to Australian cases and legislation only — it does not attempt to handle anything else.
  • International materials, treaties, and Hansard have zero tool support across every existing citation manager.
  • Two AI-generated community patches now circulate on GitHub because the official style cannot produce the dual citations that Australian courts require.

Existing tools cover about 30% of AGLC4

  • The UTS EndNote style requires manual cross-reference insertion for every subsequent reference — every (n X) must be hand-entered and refreshed with F9 before submission.
  • Parallel citations, required by every Australian court, cannot be produced by any existing tool.
  • Universities are publishing divergent institutional workarounds: Macquarie’s LibGuide tells users to ignore an entire section of the official UTS guidance.

AGLC5 is being drafted without implementer input

  • The AGLC5 Committee is in drafting following its 2023–2025 public consultation period.
  • No public submission has yet advocated for software-first design of citation rules.
  • Obiter has published an open letter requesting early access to draft rules so that the fifth edition can be the first one designed with implementability as a constraint.

How It Works

Three steps from installation to your first properly formatted citation.

1

Install

Add Obiter to Microsoft Word from AppSource. It appears as a task pane alongside your document, ready when you are.

2

Insert Citation

Select a source type, fill in the fields, and click Insert. Obiter formats the footnote and inserts it at your cursor position.

3

Write

Continue writing. Obiter manages ibid, cross-references, and short titles as your document grows. Generate a bibliography when you are done.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the AGLC?

The Australian Guide to Legal Citation — the AGLC — is the citation standard used across Australian law schools, law journals, and courts. It governs how cases, legislation, treaties, journal articles, and books are referenced in footnotes and bibliographies.

The current edition is the fourth, AGLC4, published in 2018. A fifth edition, AGLC5, is in drafting. Obiter implements AGLC4 in full and is built to adopt AGLC5 once it is published.

Does Obiter work with MS Word?

Yes. Obiter is a Microsoft Word add-in. It runs in Word for Windows, Word for Mac, Word for iPad, and Word on the web. Install it from Microsoft AppSource and it appears as a task pane beside your document — there is nothing to install on your computer beyond MS Word itself.

Is Obiter an add-in, an add-on, or a plugin?

All three names describe the same thing. Microsoft calls them Office Add-ins; in practice people say Word add-on or Word plugin just as often. Obiter is an Office Add-in for Microsoft Word, distributed through AppSource and also available as a manual sideload.

Is there a free AGLC citation generator for Word?

Yes. Obiter is a free, open-source AGLC citation and referencing tool for Microsoft Word, licensed under GPLv3. There is no subscription and no account is required to use it.

What does the tool actually do?

Obiter inserts AGLC citations and footnotes across 80+ source types, resolves subsequent references automatically (ibid, above n, and short titles), generates the bibliography, and validates every reference in the document against the rules.

It also handles material that other tools leave to manual work: parallel citations, treaties, Hansard, and 12 foreign jurisdictions.

How is this different from EndNote or Zotero?

The AGLC styles available for existing citation managers cover roughly 30% of the guide, and the official Zotero style was deliberately scoped to Australian cases and legislation only. Subsequent references generally have to be inserted and refreshed by hand.

Obiter implements the AGLC directly inside Word rather than mapping it onto a general-purpose reference manager, so ibid, cross-references, and parallel citations are handled as you write.
