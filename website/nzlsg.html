New Zealand Law Style Guide

NZLSG 3 referencing in Microsoft Word: footnotes in the general or commercial style, a grouped bibliography and New Zealand checks.

NZLSG 3 in Obiter

The New Zealand Law Style Guide is the citation guide for New Zealand courts, universities and publishers. Its third edition, NZLSG 3, was published by the Law Foundation in 2019. Obiter implements NZLSG 3 alongside the AGLC and OSCOLA.

Choose the standard from the Citation standard control in Settings. The choice is stored in the document, and the whole document is re-rendered on the next refresh. The Insert and Edit forms show the fields NZLSG reads (a New Zealand case takes its court identifier and decision number, or a file number for a case decided before neutral citations), and Record details links New Zealand cases to NZLII.

What Obiter formats under NZLSG 3

Rule numbers below are NZLSG 3’s own.

Cases

The neutral citation whenever one exists, then a comma and the best report (rr 3.1–3.3); the court identifier only for cases without a neutral citation (r 3.2.7); and earlier unreported cases by court, file number and date (r 3.4). Pinpoints take “at”, as in at [29] or at 398 (r 3.2.8).

Māori Land Court and Waitangi Tribunal

Māori Land Court decisions by block name and minute book, with the abbreviated citation (r 3.5), and Waitangi Tribunal reports by title and Wai number (r 3.6). Te reo Māori names keep their macrons.

Legislation

Short title and year in roman, with the provision after a comma, such as Crimes Act 1961, s 59 (r 4.1.1); Te Tiriti o Waitangi and the Treaty of Waitangi by article (r 4.1.1(e)); bills with their bill number (r 4.2.1); and regulations, rules and orders (r 4.3.1).

Subsequent references: general and commercial styles

NZLSG does not use ibid (r 2.3.1). In the general style a footnote gives the pinpoint alone where the source is obvious, and otherwise the identifier with an above n cross-reference, such as R v Wang, above n 49, at 533; legislation repeats the short title without the year. The Citation style control in Settings switches the document to the commercial style, which drops the above n cross-reference and gives the identifier and pinpoint only. The choice is stored in the document.

Quotations

Double quotation marks with single marks inside; quotations of 30 words or more set as an indented block without marks (r 1.2.2).

Secondary, parliamentary and international sources

Texts with the place of publication (r 6.1.1); chapters (r 6.2); articles with the title in double quotes (r 6.4); theses (r 6.7.1); internet materials without an access date (r 7.1.1); podcasts (r 7.1.8); newspapers (r 7.2); the New Zealand Parliamentary Debates (r 5.1.1); cabinet papers and the Gazette (r 5.2); Law Commission reports (r 5.2.3); and treaties, ICJ cases and UN documents (rr 10.1, 10.2, 10.4).

Bibliography

Grouped by source type with cases and legislation first, names as written rather than inverted, and every entry closed with a full stop (Appendix 7).

Validation

The NZLSG checks run, including ibid, the cross-reference style, italicised legislation and missing macrons in common te reo Māori terms such as Māori, whānau and hapū. The AGLC-only checks stay silent.

Generative AI and the next edition

Generative AI. NZLSG 3 has no rule for citing the output of a generative AI tool. Until it does, Obiter renders such a citation in its AGLC form under NZLSG, and the question is recorded for researchers rather than guessed.

NZLSG 4. A fourth edition has not been published, so Obiter does not offer it. When it is published, Obiter will compare it with NZLSG 3 rule by rule and follow the new edition.

How NZLSG support is checked

NZLSG 3 was reviewed against the published guide in September 2026. Rule notes derived from the guide drive a feature-matrix test suite that runs alongside the core AGLC4 engine, and a coverage test fails if any source type lacks a declared treatment under NZLSG.

Where NZLSG has no form of its own for a source type, such as an Australian-only source, Obiter uses the AGLC4 form. A small number of secondary source types, such as speeches, press releases and dictionaries, still render in their AGLC4 form while their NZLSG forms are built. Frequently Asked Questions

Does Obiter support the New Zealand Law Style Guide?

Yes. Obiter implements the New Zealand Law Style Guide, 3rd edition (NZLSG 3), including both the general and the commercial citation styles. Choose it per document in Settings.

Does Obiter support NZLSG 4?

Not yet, because the 4th edition has not been published. Obiter supports NZLSG 3 and will compare a 4th edition against NZLSG 3 rule by rule once it is published.

How do I cite the Waitangi Tribunal in NZLSG?

Under NZLSG 3 r 3.6 a Waitangi Tribunal report is cited with Waitangi Tribunal as the author, the report title in italics and the Wai number and year in brackets. Obiter produces that form when the report’s Wai number is entered.
